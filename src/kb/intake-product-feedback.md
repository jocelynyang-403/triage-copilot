# Handling Product Feedback and Feature Requests

This runbook defines how Meridian Cloud, Inc. handles inbound praise, feature suggestions, and general product feedback. Support owns first-touch acknowledgement; feedback is routed to Engineering, which owns the product roadmap.

## Scope

This process covers non-actionable inbound sentiment: compliments about a recent release, suggestions for new capabilities, and general opinions about how the product works. It does not cover defects, which follow the bug-report runbook, or commercial requests, which go to Sales.

## Where Feedback Is Logged

Every piece of feedback is captured in Productboard, the team's product-intelligence tool. Support tags each entry with the theme (for example, reporting, mobile, integrations), the customer's plan tier, and the account size, so Engineering can weigh demand by segment. Duplicate suggestions are linked to a single insight rather than logged separately, which keeps the signal clean when the product team reviews trends.

## Whether the Sender Gets a Reply

Yes. Every identifiable sender receives an acknowledgement within one business day. The acknowledgement thanks them for the input, confirms it has been logged for the product team, and sets honest expectations. It must not promise that a suggestion will be built, commit to a timeline, or hint at unreleased plans, because roadmap decisions are made by Engineering on a quarterly cycle and premature commitments create support debt. Praise receives a brief, warm thank-you and is still logged so positive signal reaches the team.

## How It Reaches the Product Team

Engineering reviews the Productboard backlog every two weeks and conducts a deeper prioritization review each quarter. High-frequency requests, or requests concentrated among Enterprise accounts, are surfaced for roadmap consideration. When a suggestion is later shipped, the product team can notify the original requesters through the linked insight, closing the loop.

## Ownership

Support owns acknowledgement and accurate logging; Engineering owns triage, prioritization, and any roadmap decision.
